### Provider Reclamation

* Scott Carruthers explained that Provider Reclamation introduces a grace period for tenants and deployers before a provider can close an active lease
* Explained that deployers can specify a reclamation window directly within their SDL
* Provided an example where a deployer requests a twenty-four-hour reclamation window
* Explained that when a reclamation window is included in an SDL:
  * The deployment order is submitted to the blockchain
  * Only providers supporting reclamation can bid on the workload
  * Providers must support a reclamation period equal to or greater than the period requested by the deployer
* Clarified that a workload requesting a twenty-four-hour reclamation window will only receive bids from providers supporting at least twenty-four hours of reclamation
* Explained that providers cannot close the lease until the defined reclamation period expires
* Highlighted that the feature is particularly important for production and enterprise-grade workloads
* Explained that Provider Reclamation protects deployers during:
  * Hardware decommissioning
  * Provider decommissioning
  * Maintenance periods
  * Infrastructure changes
* Noted that deployers now receive guaranteed advance notice before an eligible provider closes their lease
* Explained that the grace period gives deployers sufficient time to transition workloads gracefully
* Confirmed that not all providers currently support reclamation
* Noted that several prominent GPU providers have already enabled the feature
* Expected additional providers to adopt reclamation over time

### BME Oracle Version Two

* Scott Carruthers discussed the introduction of BME Oracle Version Two during the recent network upgrade
* Explained that the update primarily operates in the background and may not be immediately visible to network users
* Highlighted that the upgrade represents an important improvement to Burn Mint Equilibrium stability
* Explained that the Oracle previously calculated aggregated pricing using block-based measurements
* Confirmed that Oracle Version Two now uses time-period-based pricing measurements
* Explained that the previous Oracle implementation occasionally produced stale price events
* Noted that stale pricing could trigger the BME circuit breaker
* Explained that these circuit breaker events typically lasted approximately two to three minutes
* Clarified that the issue was largely transparent to normal network users
* Noted that users attempting to mint ACT during a circuit breaker window could potentially encounter the issue
* Explained that Akash Console users generally would not notice these short interruptions
* Highlighted that internal monitoring and alerting systems were identifying approximately five to ten stale-price circuit breaker events per day before the upgrade

### BME Stability Improvements

* Scott Carruthers confirmed that there had been zero stale-price circuit breaker events since the deployment of BME Oracle Version Two
* Explained that the new pricing methodology has significantly improved Oracle reliability
* Confirmed that BME is now considerably more stable from an Oracle pricing perspective
* Stated that both Provider Reclamation and Oracle Version Two are operating successfully approximately two weeks after deployment
* Described the network upgrade objectives as successfully achieved
