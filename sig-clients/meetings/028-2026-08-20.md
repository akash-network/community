## Meeting Notes

### Opening Remarks & SIG Clients

* Tyler Wright welcomed participants and explained that SIG Clients provides a forum for projects and teams building applications and client experiences on top of Akash Network.
* Participation has recently been lower as several community projects are being developed privately, and the meeting cadence or structure may be reconsidered if participation remains low.
* Community developers were encouraged to use the SIG Clients channel to share projects, ideas, questions, and product feedback.

### Akash Console Updates

* The Console team has introduced several improvements, including a redesigned onboarding experience aimed at making Akash easier for new users.
* Bid engine updates and bid pre-screening now give users an early indication of how many providers may be able to support a workload before bids arrive.
* Bid pre-screening can also help users identify potential SDL configuration problems when expected provider bids are unavailable.
* Additional improvements are underway for the Deployments and Billing pages, with some changes being tested behind feature flags before wider release.

### Console & Console Air

* Tyler reiterated that Akash Console and Console Air now serve different user groups.
* Akash Console is designed for users who prefer credit card payments and managed wallets, while Console Air provides the self-custodied crypto wallet experience.
* Both products have dedicated repositories for issues, development updates, and community contributions.
* The Core Team continues to improve the overall design and user experience across both products.

### Akash Home Node

* Akash Home Node is currently in public beta and provides a simpler way for users to bring smaller GPU clusters onto Akash without operating a full Provider.
* Users can contribute one or two nodes while the Home Node team handles cluster management and deployment operations, with revenue shared between management and resource owners.
* Participants were encouraged to join the dedicated Home Node beta Discord channel to test the product and provide real-time feedback.

### AkashML

* Tyler highlighted continued growth in AkashML, a product built on top of Akash Network for easier access to open-source AI models.
* OpenRouter remains an important distribution channel, while the team is exploring additional ways to distribute AkashML.
* AkashML has recently reached all-time highs in token usage, demonstrating growing demand and the ability of Akash Marketplace to reliably support AI workloads.

### Akash CLI Improvements

* The Core Team is actively improving the Akash CLI, with additional functionality and documentation updates expected soon.
* The updated CLI covers functionality relevant to developers, nodes, and Providers and can support teams building products on top of Akash.
* Joe Deng is looking for community contributors to help test new CLI functionality.
* Developers were encouraged to provide feature requests and feedback through SIG Clients or relevant community channels.

### Community Projects & Participation

* Tyler invited developers building open-source projects on Akash to participate in SIG Clients and share their work with the broader community.
* He acknowledged that some teams prefer to develop products in stealth but emphasized the benefits of collaboration and sharing technical feedback.
* With SIG Clients now operating on a bi-monthly schedule, the meeting structure may move toward more asynchronous or ad hoc discussions if live participation remains low.
* Console-related support topics can also be discussed through SIG Support when appropriate.

### Closing Remarks

* Tyler thanked Rodri R for participating and encouraged developers to continue sharing feedback through SIG Clients.
* Questions and product discussions can also be raised during future Steering Committee meetings or relevant SIG sessions.
* The meeting concluded after approximately fourteen minutes.
