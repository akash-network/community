## Meeting Notes

### Opening & Community Coordination

* Tyler Wright opened the monthly Steering Committee meeting and explained that it serves as a culmination of discussions across Akash Special Interest Groups and Working Groups.
* The Steering Committee provides a forum for reviewing project boards, community initiatives, GitHub discussions, grievances, and issues that need broader community attention.
* Several contributors and Core team members were unavailable because of conferences and other commitments, but the meeting proceeded to maintain a public record and provide community updates.
* Tyler reminded contributors that GitHub remains a major source of truth, with separate repositories covering Console, Console Air, Support, community activities, SIGs, Working Groups, and broader project tracking.
* Community members seeking Community Pool funding or proposing new ecosystem initiatives were encouraged to begin through public GitHub discussions.

### GPU Supply & Provider Sourcing

* Supply expansion remains a major priority as demand continues to grow for high-performance GPUs such as H200s and B300s.
* Akash representatives were attending **Data Cloud 2026** in Austin, Texas, alongside other industry events, to connect with AI developers, cloud companies, data centers, and infrastructure providers.
* The ecosystem is increasingly focused on matching large compute requirements with data centers capable of bringing the required resources onto Akash.
* Joe Deng highlighted the newly launched **Provider Search Program**, designed to incentivize community participation in identifying new GPU suppliers and data centers.
* Community members can receive a **$50 bounty** for identifying qualifying data centers that directly own their compute resources.
* An additional **$1,000 bounty** is available when a sourced data center subsequently signs a deal through the program.
* Contributors with relationships to Tier 3 or Tier 4 data centers and operators with high-performance GPUs were encouraged to participate and coordinate with Joe and Akash Club.

### Website, Documentation & Community Programs

* The Website Working Group continues meeting every two weeks to coordinate development of the open-source Akash website and documentation.
* Contributors with front-end development, design, documentation, or marketing experience were encouraged to participate through Discord and GitHub.
* The Student Ambassador Program is expanding as students return to campus, with additional universities joining the initiative.
* The website's Community Contributions section has been updated with additional Student Ambassador content and categorization.
* Contributors were encouraged to create deployment videos and educational guides covering Console, Console Air, and general Akash usage.
* Additional community and ecosystem events are expected to be added to the calendar for October, November, and December.
* Joe confirmed that monthly Fireside Chats are continuing to give community members direct access to engineers and developers working within the ecosystem.

### AEPs & Core Engineering

* Tyler encouraged community members to review **Akash Enhancement Proposals (AEPs)**, which serve as specifications for upcoming network and product improvements.
* Several AEPs remain in draft and are open to community feedback before implementation.
* Current work includes **Compute Offers**, virtual machine support, bare-metal capabilities, ARM64 support, TEE functionality, and provider verification.
* ARM64 support is undergoing final testing, while the Core team continues resolving edge cases associated with TEE providers already operating on mainnet.
* AEP 86 covers provider verification improvements and is entering its initial implementation phase.
* Provider Console is expected to receive interface and functionality changes as provider verification capabilities are integrated.
* The team also intends to simplify Provider Console by reconsidering which features are most valuable to its primary users.
* Technical contributors were encouraged to participate in AEP discussions, testing, implementation, and provider release validation.

### Console & Funding Experience

* The Client team recently launched improvements to the Akash Console funding experience.
* The changes simplify funding deployments and wallets while abstracting some of the complexity associated with escrow.
* Users can more easily add additional funds to existing deployments and manage their overall Console balances.
* Community members using the updated experience were encouraged to provide feedback through the Console Discord channel.
* Akash Club members and Insiders were encouraged to help communicate the improved funding experience to new users.

### Home Node Beta

* Home Node has completed its initial alpha testing and moved into the beta phase.
* Home Node is designed for GPU owners who want to earn from their hardware without operating and maintaining a traditional Akash provider.
* Supported hardware discussed during the meeting included the 3090, 4090, 5090, and Pro 6000.
* Eligible users can deploy through Home Node and use its front-end dashboard to monitor their participation.
* Tyler highlighted growing community participation, particularly from 5090 owners, alongside strong marketplace demand for Pro 6000 resources.

### Compute Offers & Marketplace Development

* Sandeep asked for an update on the previously discussed Marketplace v2 design and its roadmap.
* Tyler clarified that **Marketplace v2 has been renamed Compute Offers** because the scope extends beyond a marketplace redesign.
* Compute Offers is expected to encompass broader resource types and marketplace functionality, including virtual machines and bare metal.
* The AEP was in draft and expected to become publicly available for community feedback around implementation, design, and timing.
* Some development work has already taken place to demonstrate and validate parts of the proposed functionality.
* Tyler explained that large-scale implementation requires further community feedback because the wider structure of Compute Offers needs to be carefully designed.
* No definitive delivery timeline was provided for virtual machines or bare-metal support.
* Sandeep and other technical contributors were invited to participate in early sandbox and development-environment testing when the features become available.

### Roadmap Review

* Sandeep asked whether Akash remained aligned with the existing 2026 roadmap and whether previously published AEP timelines would be updated.
* Tyler explained that several roadmap items have already been completed but are not accurately reflected on the website because of issues with how completed AEP information is being synchronized.
* Completed work includes the separation of Console and Console Air and other items originally associated with earlier quarters.
* The roadmap is expected to receive a broader refresh for Q3 and Q4 as additional Core team priorities and AEPs are finalized.
* Provider Console changes, provider notifications, verification features, and other roadmap items will be discussed further during upcoming SIG Providers sessions.
* Sandeep requested updated estimates and dates for existing AEPs so the community has clearer expectations.
* Tyler agreed that updated roadmap information is needed and committed to raising the issue through the Website Working Group.

### GitHub Discussions & Community Proposals

* Tyler noted that several community discussions were active, although none had completed the SIG or Working Group process required for detailed Steering Committee consideration.
* The **Shoestring** proposal was specifically highlighted as receiving substantial community attention.
* Contributors were encouraged to carefully review proposals, ask questions, and provide feedback publicly rather than limiting discussions to private conversations with individual Core team members.
* Tyler emphasized that community proposals should progress transparently through GitHub before being escalated to the Steering Committee.

### Privacy-Preserving Compute Payments

* Austin asked whether Akash has plans to introduce privacy-preserving payment mechanisms for compute bidding, rentals, and escrow.
* His concern focused on reducing the visible association between on-chain payers and compute transactions while preserving the existing payment model.
* Tyler said he was not aware of an existing Core team roadmap item addressing private payments and asked Austin to explain the specific use case and proposed approach.
* Austin described a potential system using the **CosmWasm virtual machine and zero-knowledge proof verification** to introduce a shielded payment domain while maintaining compatibility with unshielded transactions.
* Austin indicated that he already has local demonstrations of the concept and is interested in contributing independently.
* Tyler encouraged him to formalize the work through a public GitHub discussion so the community and technical contributors can evaluate the proposal.
* Tyler explained that proposals seeking Community Pool support should demonstrate clear value to the wider Akash community.
* Depending on the proposal's scope, the work could either become a community-funded initiative or develop as an independent product that abstracts some complexity from Akash users.
* Tyler suggested SIG Clients as one possible forum for deeper technical discussion but noted that another suitable SIG or Working Group could be identified sooner.
* If the proposal receives sufficient community interest and technical discussion, it could later progress to Steering Committee consideration.

### Closing Remarks

* Tyler reiterated that the Akash roadmap needs to be refreshed to reflect completed work, new AEPs, and updated expectations for upcoming initiatives.
* The roadmap issue will be raised with the Website Working Group to ensure the public website remains accurate.
* Community members were encouraged to remain active across Discord, GitHub Discussions, SIGs, and Working Groups.
* Students were encouraged to participate in the Student Ambassador Program, while other contributors can join Akash Club or contribute independently.
* Community members were also encouraged to report documentation and website issues and participate in upcoming events and meetups.
* Austin was encouraged to publish his privacy proposal so the team could direct it to the appropriate SIG or Working Group for deeper discussion.
* The meeting concluded with a reminder that another Steering Committee meeting would take place toward the end of the month.
