## Meeting Notes

### Opening Remarks & Community Feedback

* Tyler Wright welcomed participants and explained that the Steering Committee brings together updates and discussions from Akash SIGs and Working Groups.
* The meeting time may be moved earlier in future months to accommodate more global community members, with no objections raised during the session.
* Community members were encouraged to raise agenda items first through the relevant SIG or Working Group before bringing them to the Steering Committee.

### Akash Console Updates

* The redesigned Console onboarding experience has been live for several weeks and aims to make Akash easier for new users.
* Pre-bid screening has also been released, helping users understand potential provider availability before receiving bids.
* Feedback from community members and recent hackathons continues to inform improvements to the Console experience.

### Provider Services v0.16.2

* Provider Services v0.16.2 was released with bug fixes and support for GPU interconnects for multi-node GPU workloads.
* Providers were encouraged to upgrade promptly, either through the CLI or Provider Console.
* Rodri R confirmed that the upgrade was available through Provider Console, providing an easier upgrade path for operators.

### Confidential Compute

* Phase One of Confidential Compute continues to be tested using Kata Containers.
* A Mainnet provider has been running Confidential Compute successfully for several weeks while the Core Team monitors performance.
* Additional edge cases are being tested before another Provider release enables broader support.

### BME & AKT Price Feeds

* The Core Team continues to harden Burn Mint Equilibrium and improve AKT price feed reliability following issues involving Wormhole.
* Validators expedited several governance proposals within a short period to support the required price-feed changes.
* The new feeds were operating as expected, while additional redundancy was being explored, including discussions with Chainlink.
* The objective is to reduce the likelihood of BME entering circuit-breaker mode because of price-feed failures.

### Home Node Beta

* Home Node is moving from alpha testing toward a broader public beta after more than a month of testing with real users.
* The product allows users with supported GPUs to contribute one or two nodes without operating a full Akash Provider.
* Home Node manages the infrastructure and deployments while resource owners receive payouts through the platform.
* Additional beta announcements were expected shortly after the Steering Committee meeting.

### Provider Verification & ARM64

* ARM64 support and Phase One of Provider Verification were both undergoing testing.
* Community members interested in testing these features were encouraged to participate through SIG Providers.
* The Core Team plans to continue using community providers to validate new Provider functionality before broader releases.

### Community, Events & Brand Updates

* Greg Osuri was representing Akash at AI4, one of the major AI conferences in the United States, with the objective of developing relationships that could increase network supply and demand.
* Akash's updated brand guidelines and resources are now available for community members creating applications, visual content, and other Akash-related materials.
* Contributors were encouraged to participate in the Website and Documentation Working Group and submit improvements through the open-source repositories.

### Student Ambassadors & Community Programs

* The Fall 2026 Student Ambassador cohort was preparing to begin, with participants expected to contribute through workshops, hackathons, AkashML, and broader ecosystem education.
* Joe Deng highlighted Sandeep Narahari's work identifying potential infrastructure providers and Rodri R's work attracting new Akash and AkashML users.
* The Akash Affiliate Program remains open, although near-term community efforts are placing greater emphasis on expanding provider supply.

### Community Pool Reporting

* Tyler Wright highlighted the importance of maintaining public reporting for Community Pool spending.
* Approximately 133,000 AKT from PIP revenue sharing had recently been returned to the Community Pool.
* Additional reporting updates were expected to improve transparency around how Community Pool funds are requested, spent, and returned.

### Upcoming AEPs & Marketplace Improvements

* Several new AEPs are expected covering PIP updates, lease resizing, Virtual Machines, and Bare Metal support.
* Lease resizing is intended, in part, to address situations where users need to increase resources such as CPU or RAM after deployment.
* Community feedback will be requested once the specifications are published so implementation can proceed.

### Governance & Community Participation

* Tyler encouraged community members to publicly participate in GitHub Discussions, particularly around grants, funding requests, and new technical proposals.
* Contributors were encouraged to join SIGs and Working Groups where their interests and technical expertise align.
* The Steering Committee reiterated that public community feedback helps refine proposals before implementation or governance voting.

### Closing Remarks

* Tyler Wright thanked participants and encouraged continued involvement across SIGs, Working Groups, GitHub, and Discord.
* Participants were advised to watch for potential changes to the Steering Committee meeting time.
* The meeting concluded with a reminder that Akash's development and governance depend on active community participation.
