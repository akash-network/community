## Meeting Notes

### Analytics & Stats Updates

* Tyler explained that SIG Analytics now meets quarterly because the open-source indexer and stats infrastructure are generally stable.
* Community members can request new analytics through the Console repository, Support repository, or SIG Analytics Discord channel.
* The `stats.akash.network` page remains stable, though the Console team is considering a broader redesign.
* Tyler said a public plan for future stats improvements will be shared once the Console team completes its review.

### Provider Data & GPU Pricing

* Sandeep reported a temporary provider-page anomaly where GPU usage displayed as `4/0` instead of showing the correct total capacity.
* The issue later corrected itself and may have been caused by caching, maintenance, or a temporary backend synchronization problem.
* Tyler agreed to monitor the provider and investigate if the problem appears again.
* The group also discussed the GPU pricing page, where the label “starting at” may be misleading because the displayed figure is closer to a median or average price.
* Frequently rented low-cost GPUs may not be included in the automated pricing calculation because they are unavailable when the pricing job runs.
* Tyler suggested relabeling the metric to make clear that it represents median pricing rather than the lowest available price.

### Provider Page Access

* Sandeep noted that the provider list in the managed Console now requires users to sign in, making it harder to share available GPU supply with prospective users.
* He argued that basic provider and capacity information should remain publicly accessible, especially for enterprise users evaluating Akash.
* Tyler suggested Console Air as a temporary public alternative but agreed that directing mainstream users to a wallet-based interface could create confusion.
* The group discussed either making the Console provider page public again or adding similar provider information to the main Akash website.
* Tyler agreed to share the feedback with the Console team and explore a better public provider-discovery experience.

### Analytics Review & Community Feedback

* Tyler encouraged community members to review analytics pages and identify unclear metrics, missing network statistics, and misleading labels.
* Contributors were invited to suggest additional analytics that would help users, investors, providers, and token holders understand network growth and usage.
* Future stats-page plans and interface changes will be shared publicly for community review before implementation.

### Public Container Logs & Security

* Long asked whether publishing customer deployment logs on a provider website violates Akash rules or privacy expectations.
* Tyler explained that providers are not necessarily violating Akash protocol rules by exposing logs, but publicly displaying them creates avoidable security risks.
* Deployment logs may contain credentials, secrets, configuration data, or other sensitive information accidentally exposed by users.
* Confidential computing, private registries, mTLS, JWTs, and other deployment protections can reduce exposure, but they do not justify publishing logs publicly.
* The group agreed that logs should generally be retained privately and shared only when required for troubleshooting or support.
* Tyler committed to follow up with Long through Discord with a more complete technical answer.

### Closing Remarks

* Tyler summarized the main follow-up items around provider GPU display anomalies, pricing-page wording, public provider access, and container-log security.
* The team will continue monitoring the stats experience and collect community feedback through the SIG Analytics channel.
* The meeting concluded with plans to reconvene during the next quarterly session.
