### BME Oracle & Pyth Network Migration

* Scott Carruthers explained that upcoming oracle changes will be implemented through governance proposals and will not require another network upgrade.
* The current Burn-Mint Equilibrium pricing oracle receives its primary price information from the Pyth Network.
* Pyth previously verified pricing information through Wormhole, which uses multiple signing entities to reach consensus before prices are written on-chain.
* Frequent changes to Wormhole signer addresses created operational complications and occasional brief disruptions that required attention from the Akash Core Engineering team.
* Pyth is migrating from Wormhole verification to a new router-set consensus model that uses five signers and requires agreement from three signers.
* Pyth expects the new model to involve fewer signer rotations and provide greater operational stability.
* The migration is not specific to Akash and is part of a broader Pyth Network infrastructure change.
* Community members and users are not expected to experience any service disruption or noticeable change during the migration.

### Governance Proposal Sequence

* The oracle migration will require several governance proposals rather than one combined proposal.
* Each proposal depends on contract information created or approved through the preceding proposal.
* The first proposals will establish the required smart contracts, while subsequent proposals will connect the new contract addresses and oracle parameters.
* Approximately three or four governance proposals may be required to complete the migration.
* Validators have already been informed and are being prepared to vote quickly so the proposals can be implemented without unnecessary delay.
* Tyler encouraged validators to monitor governance activity and participate in every required vote.

### Oracle Redundancy & System Hardening

* Tyler Wright explained that previous oracle interruptions had occasionally triggered circuit-breaker protections, although the Core Engineering team resolved those issues quickly.
* The team is working to ensure that Burn-Mint Equilibrium does not remain dependent on a single price-feed provider.
* Scott confirmed that additional and redundant price feeds are expected to be introduced in the near future.
* The Core Engineering team has been discussing possible integrations with Chainlink and other oracle providers.
* New smart contracts may be developed to obtain and validate pricing information from multiple independent sources.
* The objective is to improve BME resilience, reduce single-source dependency, and provide a stronger long-term oracle architecture.
* Scott clarified that the current system is not considered to be in immediate danger while the redundant architecture is being developed.

### Revenue-Sharing Proposal & Reporting

* Isaac Grooms confirmed that the revenue-sharing proposal had passed on-chain approximately two weeks before the meeting.
* July will serve as the first formal reporting period under the approved revenue-sharing structure.
* Revenue-sharing activity will be reported quarterly, beginning from the start of the second quarter.
* Reports will explain revenue earned, amounts returned, and transfers made to the Akash Community Pool.
* Community pool contributions will be denominated and reported in AKT.
* Isaac explained that reporting in another denomination could create unfair or misleading results because token conversions and transfer timing may affect the final amounts.
* A dedicated folder has been created within the community repository to publish and monitor revenue-sharing reports over time.
