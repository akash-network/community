## Meeting Notes

### Governance & Community Pool Updates

- Tyler opened the meeting with updates on recent governance activity, noting that SIG Economics did not meet in September due to limited agenda items.
- **Proposal #341**, submitted by the DCloud Foundation (DCF), had recently passed governance and covered Hermes relayer operations.
- These operations, previously managed by Overclock Labs (OCL), are being transferred to DCF under a new operational structure.
- **Proposal #342**, focused on expanding AKT market-making liquidity, was active on-chain at the time of the meeting.
- Additional funding discussions covering Core, Client, and Support Services were also active on GitHub.
- Tyler encouraged validators and community members to review the proposals, participate in discussions, and vote on governance matters.

### Proposal #342: Market-Making Liquidity Expansion

- Isaac explained that OCL had previously secured Community Pool funding through two market-making proposals.
- The funds have been distributed across multiple market makers to support AKT liquidity across exchanges.
- Market-making agreements generally target liquidity obligations equivalent to approximately **one-third of the notional AKT value**, measured at 2% market depth on both sides of the order book.
- Isaac reported positive results in trading volume and order-book depth over recent months.
- Most AKT currently deployed through market-making arrangements originates from OCL rather than the Community Pool.
- Proposal #342 seeks additional funding to expand the number of market makers and improve overall AKT liquidity.
- Isaac also described the loan structure, under which AKT is provided to market makers alongside approximately one-year call options.
- One existing option had converted into USDT after meeting its contractual conditions, with the resulting USDT continuing to support market-making operations.
- The proposal was presented as an expansion of the existing liquidity program rather than a replacement.

### Core, Client & Support Services Funding

- Isaac outlined OCL's methodology for requesting Community Pool funding for work completed on Akash Enhancement Proposals (AEPs) and related protocol improvements.
- Funding requests are based on actual engineering and operational hours attributable to network development.
- The requests are organized into three categories:
  - **Core:** Blockchain development and supporting protocol infrastructure.
  - **Client:** Console development and customer-facing deployment improvements.
  - **Support Services:** Testing environments, AEP-related community activities, administrative oversight, and supporting operations.
- Core funding includes work associated with the Pyth contract transition and exploration of a secondary price source for the Burn-Mint Equilibrium (BME) system.
- Isaac emphasized that BME requires continued improvements to strengthen reliability and resilience.
- Client funding primarily covers the Console redesign, which aims to improve onboarding, deployment workflows, and the overall user experience.
- These funding discussions are intended to occur quarterly as additional development work is completed.
- Isaac clarified that OCL-specific business activities, including AOSML, are excluded from Community Pool reimbursement.
- Akash Home Node development is also excluded unless particular work can be directly attributed to a defined community benefit.
- The funding approach is intended to provide transparent compensation for eligible protocol development while maintaining a consistent record of network improvements across quarters.

### Proposal #341: DCloud Foundation Operations

- Isaac described Proposal #341 as the first governance proposal operating under the new DCloud Foundation funding structure.
- The proposal supports Hermes relayer operations previously handled by OCL.
- DCF is expected to take a more prominent role in managing ecosystem-level operational programs and community resources.
- Isaac indicated that ongoing infrastructure requiring recurring funding, including price relayers supporting BME, should increasingly be administered through DCF.
- The objective is to establish clearer custody, ownership, and accountability for community-funded infrastructure and operations.

### Provider Incentive Program: Q3 Revenue Report

- Isaac presented Q3 results from the **Provider Incentive Program (PIP)**, which supports the distribution of compute resources across the Akash Network.
- OCL introduced a revenue-sharing arrangement in June to reflect its role in operating and maintaining PIP-related provider infrastructure.
- Under the arrangement, **70% of distributed revenue goes to the Akash Community Pool, while 30% goes to OCL**.
- Isaac reported approximately **$585,000 in Q3 earnings** across participating on-chain PIP providers.
- Revenue distribution is based on actual earnings withdrawals rather than simply accrued balances.
- The process involves withdrawing provider earnings, converting the relevant proceeds into AKT, and distributing them through a governance wallet.
- The calculated Community Pool allocation was approximately **650,000 AKT**.
- The Community Pool actually received approximately **685,000 AKT**, reflecting an earlier July calculation error involving work-order rates and on-chain costs.
- Isaac noted that the transactions and allocations can be verified on-chain.
- The report was presented as evidence that real network compute activity is generating returns for the Community Pool while also compensating OCL for operational responsibilities and risk.
- Isaac committed to exporting and publishing the Q3 report, with Tyler agreeing to include it in the SIG Economics meeting notes.

### Market-Making Transparency & Reporting

- Andrew raised questions about which centralized and decentralized exchanges would receive liquidity under the market-making proposals.
- He referenced **Proposal #316**, which previously requested 1 million AKT from the Community Pool to maintain market-making liquidity and depth.
- Isaac explained that venues for the next round of market-making loans had not yet been finalized.
- The team intends to secure funding before completing negotiations with prospective market makers and determining where additional liquidity will be deployed.
- Andrew asked whether the specific exchanges receiving liquidity would be disclosed after deployment.
- Isaac explained that OCL has historically avoided publishing individual market-making venues to prevent influencing market conditions or creating perceptions of preferential treatment.
- However, he offered to discuss potential reporting improvements internally.
- Isaac indicated that high-level liquidity performance and service-level agreement (SLA) data could be shared without necessarily disclosing individual venues.
- He also noted that existing market-making arrangements have generally delivered liquidity above contractual SLA requirements.
- No commitment was made to publish specific exchange names.

### Closing Remarks

- Community members were encouraged to review ongoing GitHub discussions before they advance to on-chain governance.
- Tyler reiterated the importance of validator participation and voting on Community Pool proposals.
- Isaac will publish the Q3 PIP report, and Tyler will make it available alongside the meeting notes.
- Additional questions and feedback can be shared asynchronously through the SIG Economics channel.
